**Brand status:** **WORKING TITLE ONLY**. No USPTO/common-law/global trademark clearance, domain/social availability, asset rights review, or verified corporate chain of title. No assertion that a concept or name is exclusively ownable. Parent organization may appear in legal credits after review, never as player-facing faction/character/quest identity.

## Product identity

**Public working title:** **THE LAST THREAD**  
**World series:** *Vessel Chronicles* (also working, not cleared).  
**Promise:** *Thirty decisions. A world on the edge. A future you can alter.*  
**Genre:** Solo-first tactical narrative survival and turn-based strategy, with a longer-term goal of grid tactics, cooperative campaigns, branching faction politics, exploration and user-authored civic improvements.  
**First five minutes:** Start without an account → name/form choice → enter home settlement → move one step → meet a mission decision → watch clock/resources change → learn to rest at hubs. Lore remains optional for players who just want to play.

## Brand expression

**Logo concept:** **The Severed Thread**, an original incomplete orbital ring interrupted by a single braided line. Avoid HumanAIOS face/sigil, the supplied tree-serpent seal or recognizably derived franchise logos as the standalone game emblem. The first UI uses wordmarks only until original artwork rights are verified.

**Palette:** Coal navy `#070d12`, weathered iron `#1a2936`, warm copper `#edbe78`, distant cyan `#98c8d1`, warning coral `#e78f88`. Use high contrast, scalable text and distinct icon shapes; never convey state through color alone.

**Narrative voice:** Concise, grounded, mysterious. Every mission gives a **verb + tangible cost + likely consequence**. In-world claims may be false, but the player UI must accurately state what the mechanic does.

**Visual content pipeline:** Concept sketches → value studies → room blockouts → navigation readability → coherent original character silhouette guide → UI kit → animation/VFX → sound cues → documented rights and provenance per asset. No automatic ownership assumption for supplied/AI-assisted images; record author, contributor agreement, licenses, usage scope and dependency chain. Preserve credits and acknowledgments externally.

**Brand clearance & protectable work:** Search USPTO federal database, relevant state/common-law uses, gaming platforms, publisher catalogs, domains/social handles and international targets. Seek counsel for confusing similarity. Copyright can cover sufficiently original game text, code, art and audiovisual expression, but **not** general game ideas, methods or name. Register eligible works and trademarks as appropriate; written assignments/employee-work policies and contributor agreements establish chain of title, not deleting identifiers. Keep source code copyright notices in repository/credits. See copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html and uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching.

## Playing, not only reading

**MDA — Mechanics → Dynamics → Aesthetics** (Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek):
- *Mechanics* — 30-tick clock, finite supplies, 10 mission triggers, navigable graph, 3 endings, explicit transformation, fragment temptation, optional memory archive.
- *Dynamics* — players ration travel, rest instead of rushing, prioritize fragile deadlines, compare faction promises and sacrifice powers for Severance. Players can rescue workers or sabotage, rupture or reinforce roots.
- *Aesthetics* — tension, discovery, ownership of sacrifice, meaningful uncertainty and earned relief. Verify with actual playtesters, not design claims.

**Self-determination (autonomy, competence, relatedness):** meaningful player choices and clear feedback for autonomy/competence; later co-op collaboration/social bonds for relatedness. Optional helper can explain **rules** but personal Memory Archive is never a covert coach. Research consent is separate and off by default.

**Final Fantasy Tactics as a design comparison, not source assets:** aim for a future small grid-battle scenario with positioning, cover/elevation, a simple skill economy and synergy roles. Today's prototype has a **topological map**, not a tactical combat grid: don't market it as FFT-style combat yet.

**Minecraft as design comparison, not source assets:** the long-term crafting/building loop should give reusable pieces, understandable recipes, shared settlement ownership and visible player-made consequences. Today's prototype has **branching navigation, resource management, form choice**, not voxel building; no Minecraft-style creative tooling is claimed.

**The Elfstones of Shannara as a design comparison, not licensed story:** mythic awakening with costly powers, world-scale stakes, intimate relationships. Express with new original factions, character arcs, dialogue and visual symbolism, rather than copying its characters, magic objects or plot.

## Retention and test targets

**Before external launch, ask five strangers to play without tutorial help.** Track opt-in product usability results separately from research. Sample operational gates (targets, not measured achievements):

- **Time to first meaningful decision:** median < 90 seconds.
- **Understand next action:** at least 4/5 initial testers identify where they can move without instruction.
- **Complete one mission:** at least 4/5 within five minutes.
- **Discover rest/supply rule:** at least 4/5 by tick 10; UI should explain it.
- **Ending clarity:** at least 4/5 understand WHY their ending occurred and that other routes exist.
- **Zero blocker UI errors**, no soft-lock except clearly explained earned loss, resume/reset and export work.
- **Accessibility:** keyboard-only, visible focus, legible contrast, screen-reader names, mobile touch targets, reduced motion, no reliance on hue alone; recruit accessibility testers.
- **Engagement without extraction:** ask if playing was fun, tense and understandable. Do not infer real personality from choices or maximize compulsive retention.

Recommended product playtest prompts: “Where would you go now?” “Which action looks expensive?” “What surprised you?” “What would bring you back?” Preserve voluntary feedback, never covertly interrogate.

## Build/tool choices

| Stage | Tool | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Now: executable rules | Plain ECMAScript module + Node tests | Portable, deterministic, easy CI and replay; no engine migration yet |
| Current prototype UI | HTML/SVG/CSS | Playable in browser, responsive, low hardware burden, no third-party client libraries |
| Tactical battle slice | Phaser or Godot (evaluate, not selected) | Real grid, pathfinding, animation, controller options; compare licenses/export and integration |
| Multiplayer (later) | Existing first-valid-move server | Preserve 2–8 co-op and room persistence; do not introduce a second authority |
| Cloud / identity | Current first-party lineage service + approved Azure backend; consider PlayFab only after non-duplication review | Avoid duplicate identity writers and extra spend |
| Discord distribution | Embedded App SDK after staged auth checks | Additional entry point to same game, never owner of permanent character history |
| User feedback | Opt-in usability study | Research separately consented; raw dialogue/psychometric inference off |

## Product ownership gates

1. Freeze a **source-of-truth game design registry** with decision/provenance records and feature ownership.
2. Independently check title, character/location naming, artwork, sound, UI marks, campaign text and all third-party dependencies for clearance/assignment/licensing. Nothing is "owned" merely because renamed.
3. Separate **game product** legal rights, customer data permissions, and internal engine governance. Do not delete technical attribution or evidence hashes when changing a visible brand.
4. Exportable first-party player identity remains stable after removing Discord, replacing a cloud provider or ending one campaign.
5. Protect **gameplay authorship** through code, authored narrative, distinctive art/sound/animation, trade dress and potential trademarks where registrable; never claim monopoly over public philosophical concepts or basic game mechanics.
6. Keep automated code change as reviewable **proposals**; the game cannot ratify itself or deploy its own patches. No player vote bypasses merge, safety, copyright or budget gates.
