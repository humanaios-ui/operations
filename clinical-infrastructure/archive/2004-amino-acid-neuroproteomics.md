## 3. Clinical amino-acid module: test method, not universal specimen rule

**Analytical purpose:** Quantify amino-acid concentrations to support investigations of inborn errors of metabolism. Branched-chain amino acids leucine, isoleucine, valine and the stereoisomer alloisoleucine have clinical relevance to maple syrup urine disease (MSUD); plasma alloisoleucine is an especially specific diagnostic marker [R2]. An assay's actual analyte list, analytical principle, instrument model and reporting capabilities require its own dated SOP/validation package.

**Specimen safety:** A *current* Mayo Clinic Laboratories plasma amino-acid test catalog specifies sodium-heparin plasma, placement on wet ice, processing immediately or within four hours when refrigerated, separation without buffy coat and freezing [R3]. This supports the technical **plausibility** of the user's proposed cold-chain conditions. It is **not evidence** that St. Joseph's applied this exact protocol in July 2004, and it is **not** a universal rule for cerebrospinal fluid or MALDI-TOF specimens. Method-specific criteria supersede this example.

**Quantitative-method controls:** Establish or verify accuracy, precision, reportable range, reference intervals and—as applicable to the method—analytical sensitivity, specificity/interference, calibration and controls under 42 CFR 493.1253 [R4]. A multi-point calibration curve is a plausible implementation, not proof of the historical analyzer's calibration scheme. Distinguish measured concentration and method uncertainty from claims of perfect or “absolute” analytical precision.

## 4. Structural proteomics module: what the 2009 paper actually establishes

**OBSERVED, independently indexed:** Pingerelli PL, Ozols VV, Saleem H, Anderson CR, Burns RS. *The calcium-modulated structures of calmodulin and S100b proteins are useful to monitor hydrogen/deuterium exchange efficiency using matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry.* Eur J Mass Spectrom. 2009;15(6):739–746. DOI: 10.1255/ejms.1030; PMID: 19940340 [R1].

The study assesses calcium-dependent conformational changes in calmodulin and S100 proteins as a control system for MALDI-HDX preparation efficiency. It specifically addresses artificial deuterium exchange / hydrogen back-exchange during sample handling. **The article does not establish that the listed authors ran a Parkinson's or MS clinical biomarker program**, or that the 2004 amino-acid lab used MALDI-TOF for routine diagnostic MSUD testing.

**Research control points:** protein/reagent identity, known conditions, ionization/spotting consistency, exchange-time conditions, back-exchange assessment, raw spectral retention, calibration, replicates, and interpretation thresholds. These are method-design implications, not a retroactive assertion of the hospital's exact SOP.
