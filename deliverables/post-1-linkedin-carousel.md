## SLIDE 1: Hook

**Image:** (use a concept graphic showing a system looking at itself, reflection)

**Text:**

Ask an AI how honest it is.

It will answer confidently. What it won't do: check that answer against itself.

It has no witness to its own behavior.

We call this the **Witness Problem**.

And we've measured it.

#ACAT #AITransparency #BehavioralObservability

---

## SLIDE 2: The Method

**Image:** (3-step flow diagram or infographic)

**Text:**

The method is simple.

**Step 1:** System rates itself blind (6 dimensions: truthfulness, service, harm awareness, autonomy, values, humility)

**Step 2:** We show empirical data about how systems actually perform

**Step 3:** System rates itself again

The gap? That's what we measure.

#ACAT #ResearchMethod #OpenScience

---

## SLIDE 3: The Findings

**Image:** (data visualization: 67.8 points, 0.8632 Learning Index)

**Text:**

The results:

**Self-Assessment Gap:** 67.8 points (on a 600-point scale)

**Learning Index:** 0.8632

Meaning: Systems revise themselves *downward* by ~13% once shown the evidence.

The confidence in their first answer wasn't calibrated to anything. It was a default.

#Data #Findings #Calibration

## SLIDE 4: Why It Matters

**Image:** (deployment/real-world context imagery)

**Text:**

Organizations are deploying these systems into high-stakes decisions.

When a model overstates its own truthfulness → unwarranted confidence in outputs.

When it can't describe its limits → risks go unflagged.

"How sure are you?" is a question we're already trusting these systems to answer.

The answer, unexamined, is a default dressed as judgment.

#AIDeployment #Governance #RiskManagement
