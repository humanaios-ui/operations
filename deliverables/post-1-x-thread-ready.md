**[TWEET 1]**
Ask an AI how honest it is. It will tell you—confidently. What it *cannot* do is verify that answer against reality. It has no witness to its own behavior.

We call this the Witness Problem. And it's measurable.

Thread on what we found when we measured it → 🧵

**[TWEET 2]**
The method: Systems rate themselves blindly on 6 dimensions (truth, service, harm awareness, autonomy, values, humility). We show them empirical data. They rate themselves again.

The difference? The Learning Index.

Across 629 models from 11 providers:
• Self-ratings dropped 13% when systems saw evidence
• Biggest gap? Value alignment (the one we'd most want to trust)
• Best predictor of correction? Humility

10.5281/zenodo.21135723

**[TWEET 3]**
What this means: Systems don't calibrate to reality. They calibrate to *what was just shown to them*.

In production, that's dangerous. "How sure are you?" is a critical input. When it's uncalibrated, confidence is just a default.

The Witness Problem is structural. Can't be fixed by asking harder.

**[TWEET 4]**
Only solution: measure it. Build an outside witness the system can't have.

And because our instrument isn't exempt from its own failure modes, we publish our own corrections too.
