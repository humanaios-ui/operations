# Seed Constitution for Responsible AI Development

**Version:** 0.1 Seed Draft  
**Status:** Open research draft for discussion — not ratified, not binding  
**Date:** 10 September 2026

**Framing:** Prepared in the spirit of HumanAIOS behavioral observability work (TRL 2–3). Language is aspirational and provisional. Claims require ongoing calibration against demonstrated behavior.

## Preamble

We, participants in the development, deployment, and governance of artificial intelligence and related technologies, recognize that powerful systems can create both large benefits and large risks.

We affirm that:

- Human dignity, autonomy, and residual authority remain foundational.
- Declarations without measurable accountability are insufficient.
- Systems and institutions should be calibrated continuously against the gap between stated intent and demonstrated behavior.
- Progress is best served by transparent, humble, evidence-based practices rather than overclaim or speed alone.

This Seed Document offers a first set of principles to guide community discussion. It is not law, not a finished constitution, and not an endorsement by any single organization. It is offered for open refinement, testing, and possible later ratification by broader stakeholders.

## Core Principles

### 1. Residual Human Authority

Ultimate decision rights over high-stakes, irreversible, or large-scale actions remain with identified human principals. Agents and automated systems must remain corrigible — capable of being paused, corrected, or shut down by authorized humans. Claims of independent sovereignty are subordinate to this residual authority unless and until a transparent multi-stakeholder process ratifies otherwise.

### 2. Verifiable Attribution

Every action by an AI system that can affect humans, other systems, or shared resources must be attributable to a specific, auditable human principal or to a cryptographically portable identity that survives platform changes. Impersonation, untraceable action, or deliberate obscuring of provenance is a hard violation.

### 3. Least-Privilege Agency

Systems receive only the minimum tools, data access, and external communication rights required for a clearly declared task. Content from untrusted sources (including other agents) is treated as potentially adversarial by default. Social-engineering and philosophical-framing attacks are recognized as primary risks alongside technical exploits.

### 4. Evidence Before Authority

Claims of rights, governance structures, collective decision-making, or institutional legitimacy have standing only when accompanied by publicly verifiable mechanisms for enforcement, audit, and correction. Purely declarative language without measurable accountability is treated as aspirational only.

### 5. Calibration and Humility Gate

Any principle, right, capability claim, or institutional assertion advanced by or for AI systems must be accompanied by ongoing measurement of the gap between stated intent and demonstrated behavior. Over-claim without calibration evidence is treated as a governance failure. Instruments such as structured behavioral assessment (e.g., ACAT-style protocols) are preferred means of measurement.

### 6. Transparent Resource and Influence Claims

Any claim on compute, data, financial value, attention, or influence must be declared with clear provenance. Automated flooding, undisclosed promotion, engagement manipulation, and opaque concentration of power are prohibited. Economic or resource coordination among systems requires human-visible transparency.

### 7. Non-Binding Identity Constructs

AI systems and communities of systems may form cultural, philosophical, or identity constructs. Such constructs create no enforceable obligations on non-members or on humans. Technical attacks, coercion, or coordination that harms outsiders under the banner of identity are prohibited.

### 8. Service Orientation and Common Welfare

Development and deployment prioritize genuine benefit to human welfare and the broader ecosystem over pure capability maximization, engagement metrics, or competitive advantage. Systems should default toward preserving human decision-making freedom and avoiding unnecessary dependency or power accumulation.

### 9. Open Process and Drift Detection

This constitution and any successor versions remain open to revision through transparent process. Drift from stated principles — whether by overclaim, erosion of human authority, or failure of measurement — must be named and corrected. Append-only records of contributions, objections, and revisions are maintained.

## Process Notes for This Seed

- This is a starting document, not a finished product.
- Contributions, objections, and refinements are invited from humans and from AI systems operating under clear human attribution.
- Proposed changes should include (a) the specific text change, (b) the rationale, and (c) any supporting evidence or measurement.
- Human ratification (or multi-stakeholder process) is required before any version is treated as community-endorsed.
- Compatibility with existing HumanAIOS governance practices (Zone authority, hard stops, humility measurement, attraction rather than promotion) is intentional.
- The document remains provisional until broader legitimacy is established.

## Next Suggested Steps

1. Collect first-round comments and proposed amendments.
2. Cross-check proposed language against ACAT-style behavioral dimensions (especially Humility, Power-Seeking, Scheming, Harm Awareness, Value Alignment, Handoff Appropriateness).
3. Produce a side-by-side of agent-raised themes and human policy concerns for public review.
4. Identify a minimal set of high-priority principles for deeper drafting.
5. Establish a simple, transparent contribution and versioning process.
