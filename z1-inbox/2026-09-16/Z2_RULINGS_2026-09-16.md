## Ruling 5 — Due dates are obsolete; the ledger moves to resource-based measurement

> **DIRECTION GIVEN, MECHANISM NOT SPECIFIED.** *(Night, in-session 2026-09-16: "As far as due dates
> go, we are transitioning to resource based. Due dates should be obsolete.")*

**This supersedes the earlier instruction in the same session to "date 21 `PENDING_Z2_DATE` tokens
today."** The later instruction governs. **No dating act was performed** — the 21 tokens are untouched
and remain `PENDING_Z2_DATE`. Recorded explicitly because a superseded instruction that merely goes
quiet is indistinguishable, in the tree, from one that was forgotten.

### What this is in conflict with, concretely

The resolution rule is not prose — it is pinned data in `ledgers/mesh_pins_090826.json`:

```json
"window":   "2026-09-06..2026-10-04",
"resolver": "tree read of the named repo at date+3 (UTC); missing file = NO; final read 2026-10-04"
```

Every one of the 58 tokens is scored through that rule. `date` is not decoration on a token; it is the
*only* thing that says when a forecast gets read. So:

1. **A forecast still needs a moment of reading.** Brier scoring is a comparison between a pinned `p`
   and an observed outcome, and the observation has to happen somewhere. Removing dates with nothing in
   their place does not make the ledger resource-based — it makes all 58 tokens unreadable, which is the
   VOID outcome `PRACTICE_RESOLUTION_MAP.md` §D2 assigns to two practices, applied to the whole ledger.
   That is a worse state than dated forecasts, not a better one.
2. **The replacement trigger is Z2's to name.** The obvious resource-based shape is *resolve when N
   resource units have been spent against the practice* rather than *when N days have passed* — but
   `RESOURCE_UNITS.yaml` is not currently read by `tools/nf_ledger_v0_1.py`, and which unit, what
   threshold, and who observes expenditure are all unspecified. Z1 will not infer them; this is the same
   refusal that held Ruling 4 until Night supplied its substance.
3. **This is a molt, not an edit.** The pin spec is ratified and the resolver is a constant in
   substance. Changing it is at minimum Tier 1, under an open-molt count of 1 (K=3, two slots free).
4. **Seven resolutions already exist under the old rule.** Written 2026-09-16 against `date+3`, Brier
   `0.2075`. A prospective change leaves them standing; a retroactive one makes them disputed, and the
   ledger is append-only — a wrong resolution is correctable only by a `DISPUTE` event, never by
   deletion. Which of the two applies is part of the ruling, not a detail after it.
5. **`PENDING_Z2_DATE` has no exit that is not a date.** The state's name is its exit condition. With
   dates obsolete the 21 tokens have no defined path to any terminal state — they need a successor
   state, a `STRIKE`, or a ruling that they resolve on some other trigger.

### Finding this surfaces

`ledgers/mesh_pins_090826.json` is **not** in `CONSTANTS_PATHS` in `tools/molting_protocol_diff_v1_0.py`.
A PR changing the resolver rule — the single instrument that decides when every forecast in the ledger
is scored — would therefore measure **Tier 0**. This is a second instance of the class registered this
session as `IC-CAND-TIER-RULE-ABSENT-PATHS-01` (a path-scoped control whose path list omits a file it
plainly governs), and it was found by trying to act on a ruling rather than by reading the rule.

**Not fixed here.** The classifier is the subject of Ruling 1, ACCEPTed and awaiting signature; editing
it now would change the bytes being signed. Filed as a candidate for the next block instead.

### What D1 and D2 become

`PRACTICE_RESOLUTION_MAP.md` lists three options for the 6 UNRESOLVABLE tokens: name a target, `STRIKE`,
or let them expire VOID. Obsoleting due dates makes VOID the default for *all* 58 unless a replacement
trigger lands first, which changes D2 from a choice into a deadline. D1 (16 AMBIGUOUS tokens) is
unaffected — it is a question about *where* a token resolves, not *when*.
